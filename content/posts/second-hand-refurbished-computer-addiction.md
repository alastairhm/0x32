I'll admit it: I have a problem. Every time a second-hand or refurbished machine turns up cheap, I find a reason to buy it. The result is a small fleet of computers accumulating around the house, most of which I didn't strictly need.

It's not really about the hardware itself, though. It's an enjoyable side hobby — each new (old) machine is an excuse to play around with a different operating system, distro, or desktop environment without any risk to the computer I actually rely on day to day. Recently that meant putting [Omarchy Linux on an old laptop](/posts/omarchy-linux-on-an-old-laptop/) just to see how it held up.

There's a genuine work benefit to it too. Having a pile of spare machines to experiment on means I can try out cutting-edge software and tooling at home before it shows up in a work context, rather than learning it cold under pressure. It keeps me current in a low-stakes way, and second-hand or refurbished kit makes that experimentation cheap enough that I don't feel bad wiping a drive and starting over on a whim.

I doubt this habit is going away any time soon. There's always another distro to try, another oddball bit of hardware to see running Linux, and another "just one more" refurbished box going for next to nothing.
